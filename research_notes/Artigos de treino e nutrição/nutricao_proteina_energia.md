Verification method: every DOI below was resolved against OpenAlex (title/authors/journal/year/citation count/OA status match) on 2026-10-27; abstracts were pulled from Europe PMC. ## Q1 — Daily protein requirement for hypertrophy (Morton 2018, Nunes 2022, Tagawa 2021/2022, ISSN Jäger 2017)

### Takeaway
The strongest meta-analytic evidence puts the point of diminishing returns for lean-mass gain at **~1.6 g/kg/day** (Morton breakpoint 1.62 g/kg/d; upper 95% CI ~2.2 g/kg/d), with Nunes 2022 confirming benefits for <65 y only at ≥1.6 g/kg/d. A sensible app default is **1.6 g/kg/d (minimum) – 2.2 g/kg/d (upper target)**; the ISSN band is 1.4–2.0 g/kg/d.

### Cited Findings
- **Morton RW, Murphy KT, McKellar SR, Schoenfeld BJ, Henselmans M, Helms E, Aragon AA, Devries MC, Banfield L, Krieger JW, Phillips SM.** "A systematic review, meta-analysis and meta-regression of the effect of protein supplementation on resistance training-induced gains in muscle mass and strength in healthy adults." *Br J Sports Med* 2018;52(6):376–384 (online 2017). DOI [10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608).   - The 95% CI of the breakpoint is 1.03–2.20 g/kg/d (full text; widely cited as the basis for "go up to 2.2 g/kg to cover most people"). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608) (CI value from full text, not re-checked against the abstract in this session — see Gaps)

Recommends 1.2–2.0 g/kg/d protein. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000852)

### Inferences
- App default: target **1.6 g/kg/d** as floor for hypertrophy goals, allow up to **2.2 g/kg/d** (covers the Morton upper CI and most inter-individual variation). Values above 2.2 g/kg/d at maintenance/surplus are safe in healthy adults per ISSN but give minimal additional hypertrophy.
- For obese users, base on target/adjusted body weight or FFM (Morton/Nunes trials were mostly non-obese) — this is a practitioner inference, not a meta-analytic finding.

### Gaps
- Morton breakpoint 95% CI (1.03–2.20) is from my knowledge of the full text; the abstract fetched here does not print it. Verify in the PDF if it will be quoted literally.
- Jäger 2017 and Mazzulla 2020 numeric points were not re-read from the abstract in this session (Europe PMC returned errors for Jäger).
- No 2023–2026 meta-analysis found that supersedes Morton/Nunes for daily protein at energy balance.

## Q2 — Protein distribution per meal (Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018; Trommelen 2023; Areta 2013)

### Takeaway
Practical consensus: **~0.4 g/kg per meal across ≥4 meals** (to reach ≥1.6 g/kg/d), max ~0.55 g/kg/meal when aiming for 2.2 g/kg/d. Trommelen 2023 shows there is no hard "per-meal ceiling" (100 g > 25 g, anabolic response >12 h), so meal count is a convenience/optimization variable, not a hard rule — daily total matters most.

### Cited Findings
- **Schoenfeld BJ, Aragon AA.** "How much protein can the body use in a single meal for muscle-building? Implications for daily protein distribution." *J Int Soc Sports Nutr* 2018;15:10. DOI [10.1186/s12970-018-0215-1](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-018-0215-1). Narrative/critical review. **~125 citations.** OA: gold. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-018-0215-1)
  - Numbers: "consume protein at a target intake of **0.4 g/kg/meal across a minimum of four meals** in order to reach a minimum of **1.6 g/kg/day**. Using the upper daily intake of **2.2 g/kg/day** … would necessitate a maximum of **0.55 g/kg/meal**." Doses >20 g increase oxidation but not all extra AA are oxidized. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-018-0215-1)
- **Trommelen J, van Lieshout GAA, Nyakayiru J, Holwerda AM, Smeets JSJ, Hendriks FK, van Kranenburg JMX, Zorenc AH, Senden JM, Goessens JPB, Gijsen AP, van Loon LJC.** "The anabolic response to protein ingestion during recovery from exercise has no upper limit in magnitude and duration in vivo in humans." *Cell Rep Med* 2023;4(12):101324. DOI [10.1016/j.xcrm.2023.101324](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2023.101324). Acute RCT with quadruple isotope tracers (not a meta-analysis). **~80 citations** (fast-rising). OA: gold. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2023.101324)
  - Numbers: 100 g protein produced a greater and more prolonged (**>12 h**) anabolic response than 25 g; dose-dependent increase in muscle protein synthesis; negligible effect on whole-body breakdown or amino-acid oxidation. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2023.101324)
- **Areta JL, Burke LM, Ross ML, Camera DM, West DWD, Broad EM, Jeacocke NA, Moore DR, Stellingwerff T, Phillips SM, Hawley JA, Coffey VG.** "Timing and distribution of protein ingestion during prolonged recovery from resistance exercise alters myofibrillar protein synthesis." *J Physiol* 2013;591(9):2319–2331. DOI [10.1113/jphysiol.2012.244897](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.2012.244897). Acute RCT. **~490 citations.** OA: green. (Classic finding: 4×20 g every 3 h > 8×10 g or 2×40 g for 12-h MPS — from full text, not re-read here.) — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.2012.244897)
- Helms 2014 contest-prep review: 3–6 meals/day with 0.4–0.5 g/kg protein pre- and post-training; "alterations in nutrient timing and frequency appear to have little effect on fat loss or lean mass retention." — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/1550-2783-11-20)
- Iraki 2019: 0.40–0.55 g/kg per meal, 3–6 meals evenly distributed. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports7070154)

### Inferences
- Trommelen 2023 is acute (single session, 12 h, n per arm small) and does not invalidate the 0.4 g/kg × ≥4 meal guidance; it weakens the "max 20–30 g per meal or it's wasted" myth. The app can allow 2–3 large protein meals if adherence demands, while defaulting to 3–5 meals of 0.3–0.55 g/kg.

### Gaps
- No long-term RCT/meta-analysis directly shows that even vs. skewed distribution changes lean mass in trained adults at matched daily protein; evidence is mostly acute MPS. (Protein timing pre vs post is out of scope; a 2025 Nutrients MA by Casuso & Goossens found no LBM difference — noted only for context.)

## Q3 — Protein during caloric deficit (lean-mass preservation)

### Takeaway
During a deficit, protein needs rise: **1.6–2.4 g/kg/d** (Hector & Phillips) or **2.3–3.1 g/kg FFM/d** (Helms 2014) scaled up with leanness and deficit severity. The newest meta-regression (Refalo, Trexler & Helms 2025) finds a linear dose-response with no clear plateau, strongest when expressed per kg FFM, in lean men, and in trials >4 wk.

### Cited Findings
- **Helms ER, Zinn C, Rowlands DS, Brown SR.** "A Systematic Review of Dietary Protein During Caloric Restriction in Resistance Trained Lean Athletes: A Case for Higher Intakes." *Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab* 2014;24(2):127–138. DOI [10.1123/ijsnem.2013-0054](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2013-0054). Systematic review (6 studies). **~125 citations.** OA: closed. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2013-0054)
  - Numbers: "Protein needs for energy-restricted resistance-trained athletes are likely **2.3–3.1 g/kg of FFM**, scaled upwards with severity of caloric restriction and leanness." Only the lean, high-deficit group that preserved FFM ate 2.5–2.6 g/kg. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2013-0054)
- **Hector AJ, Phillips SM.** "Protein Recommendations for Weight Loss in Elite Athletes: A Focus on Body Composition and Performance." *Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab* 2018;28(2):170–177. DOI [10.1123/ijsnem.2017-0273](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2017-0273). Narrative review. **~135 citations.** OA: bronze. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2017-0273)
  - Numbers: "Current recommendations for protein intakes during weight loss in athletes are set at **1.6–2.4 g protein/kg/day**"; position in range depends on deficit severity and training. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.2017-0273)
- **Longland TM, Oikawa SY, Mitchell CJ, Devries MC, Phillips SM.** "Higher compared with lower dietary protein during an energy deficit combined with intense exercise promotes greater lean mass gain and fat mass loss: a randomized trial." *Am J Clin Nutr* 2016;103(3):738–746. DOI [10.3945/ajcn.115.119339](https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.115.119339). RCT, n=40 young men, 4 wk, ~40% deficit, RT+HIIT 6 d/wk. **~270 citations.** OA: bronze. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.115.119339)
  - Numbers: **2.4 g/kg/d** vs 1.2 g/kg/d → greater LBM gain and fat loss despite ~40% deficit. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.3945/ajcn.115.119339)
- **Refalo MC, Trexler ET, Helms ER.** "Effect of Dietary Protein on Fat-Free Mass in Energy Restricted, Resistance-Trained Individuals: An Updated Systematic Review With Meta-Regression." *Strength Cond J* 2025. DOI [10.1519/SSC.0000000000000888](https://doi.org/10.1519/SSC.0000000000000888). Bayesian SR + meta-regression, 29 studies. ~6 citations (new). OA: hybrid; AUT repository copy [here](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/handle/10292/18694). — [Source](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/1aa54baf-6885-4739-aba7-df0087c3eae8)
  - Numbers: >97% probability of a **linear** dose-response (β = 0.07 per g/kg BM; 0.06 per g/kg FFM); stronger per FFM, >4 wk, in men, lower BF%. Authors call findings "exploratory" due to heterogeneity. A secondary blog summary claims benefits continued up to ~3.2 g/kg BM / 4.2 g/kg FFM — not verified against full text. — [Source](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/1aa54baf-6885-4739-aba7-df0087c3eae8); [blog (unverified)](https://aspire-fit-llc.squarespace.com/fitness-vault/4pydya9iowenyl2hbyocx8jmvknke1)
- **Aragon AA, Schoenfeld BJ, Wildman R, Kleiner S, VanDusseldorp T, Taylor L, Earnest CP, Arciero PJ, et al.** "International society of sports nutrition position stand: diets and body composition." *J Int Soc Sports Nutr* 2017;14:16. DOI [10.1186/s12970-017-0174-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-017-0174-y). Position stand. **~300 citations.** OA: gold. (States 2.3–3.1 g/kg FFM may be needed for lean RT individuals in a deficit — from my knowledge of the text; not re-read.) — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12970-017-0174-y)

### Inferences
- App rule: in a cut, default **2.0–2.4 g/kg BW/d** (or 2.3–3.1 g/kg FFM if body-fat estimate available); raise toward the top for lean users (e.g., men <15% BF, women <25% BF — inference) and aggressive deficits. Refalo 2025 supports "more is not harmful and may help" during energy restriction, unlike at maintenance.

### Gaps
- Refalo 2025 full text (and any explicit plateau/upper value) not accessed; treat the 3.2 g/kg figure as unverified.

## Q4 — Energy deficit size and rate of weight loss

### Takeaway
Lose **~0.5–1.0% body weight per week** (Helms 2014); slower (~0.7%/wk) preserved/increased LBM better than ~1.4%/wk in athletes (Garthe 2011). Meta-regression (Murphy & Koehler 2022) suggests a deficit **≥~500 kcal/day** prevents lean-mass gains; strength is preserved.

### Cited Findings
- **Murphy C, Koehler K.** "Energy deficiency impairs resistance training gains in lean mass but not strength: A meta-analysis and meta-regression." *Scand J Med Sci Sports* 2022;32(1):125–137 (online 2021). DOI [10.1111/sms.14075](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14075). SR/MA + meta-regression of RCTs ≥3 wk. **~60 citations.** OA: hybrid. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14075)
  - Numbers: LM gains impaired in RT+deficit vs RT+control (ES −0.57, p=0.02); strength comparable (ES −0.31, p=0.28). Meta-regression: a deficit of **~500 kcal/day** prevented LM gains; authors advise those preserving LM during weight loss to **avoid deficits >500 kcal/day**. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1111/sms.14075); [summary](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/node?id=1632530)
- **Helms ER, Aragon AA, Fitschen PJ.** "Evidence-based recommendations for natural bodybuilding contest preparation: nutrition and supplementation." *J Int Soc Sports Nutr* 2014;11:20. DOI [10.1186/1550-2783-11-20](https://doi.org/10.1186/1550-2783-11-20). Narrative review/recommendations. **~225 citations.** OA: gold. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/1550-2783-11-20)
  - Numbers: "Caloric intake should be set at a level that results in bodyweight losses of approximately **0.5 to 1%/wk** to maximize muscle retention"; protein **2.3–3.1 g/kg LBM/d**. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/1550-2783-11-20)
- **Garthe I, Raastad T, Refsnes PE, Koivisto A, Sundgot-Borgen J.** "Effect of Two Different Weight-Loss Rates on Body Composition and Strength and Power-Related Performance in Elite Athletes." *Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab* 2011;21(2):97–104. DOI [10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97). RCT, n=24 elite athletes, 4 RT sessions/wk. **~210 citations.** OA: closed. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97)
  - Numbers: slow (target 0.7%/wk; energy intake −19%) vs fast (1.4%/wk; −30%); both lost ~5.5% BW; LBM **+2.1%** in slow vs −0.2% in fast; conclusion: aim for **~0.7% BW/week**. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97)
- **Roberts BM, Helms ER, Trexler ET, Fitschen PJ.** "Nutritional Recommendations for Physique Athletes." *J Hum Kinet* 2020;71:79–108. DOI [10.2478/hukin-2019-0096](https://doi.org/10.2478/hukin-2019-0096). Narrative review (updates Helms 2014 + Iraki 2019). **~65 citations.** OA: diamond. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.2478/hukin-2019-0096)
- **Garthe I, Raastad T, Refsnes PE, Sundgot-Borgen J.** "Effect of nutritional intervention on body composition and performance in elite athletes." *Eur J Sport Sci* 2013;13(3):295–303. DOI [10.1080/17461391.2011.643923](https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391.2011.643923). RCT (surplus arm; see Q5). ~85 citations. OA: bronze. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391.2011.643923)

### Inferences
- App rule: default deficit = whatever produces **0.5–1.0% BW/wk** (≈ −10 to −25% of TDEE); cap around **−500 kcal/day** for lean users who want to retain/gain muscle, allowing faster loss (up to ~1%/wk) for higher-body-fat users (inference: higher BF tolerates larger deficits, consistent with Helms 2014 IJSNEM's "scaled with leanness"). Adjust intake from weekly weigh-in trend rather than trusting the TDEE equation.

### Gaps
- The Murphy & Koehler ~500 kcal threshold derives from a small meta-regression across heterogeneous populations (many untrained/overweight); it's a heuristic, not a precise cutoff.

## Q5 — Caloric surplus for muscle gain ("bulking")

### Takeaway
Evidence is thin; best guidance: **~10–20% surplus** (Iraki 2019) aiming for **~0.25–0.5% BW/week** for novice/intermediate, smaller for advanced. Helms 2023 RCT: a 15% surplus mainly increased fat (skinfolds) without clear extra muscle vs 5%/maintenance — supporting **small surpluses (~5–10%)** for trained lifters.

### Cited Findings
- **Iraki J, Fitschen P, Espinar S, Helms E.** "Nutrition Recommendations for Bodybuilders in the Off-Season: A Narrative Review." *Sports* 2019;7(7):154. DOI [10.3390/sports7070154](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports7070154). Narrative review. **~80 citations.** OA: gold. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports7070154)
  - Numbers: hyper-energetic diet **~10–20%**, target gain **~0.25–0.5% BW/week** (novice/intermediate); advanced more conservative; protein **1.6–2.2 g/kg/d**, 0.40–0.55 g/kg/meal, 3–6 meals. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports7070154)
- **Slater GJ, Dieter BP, Marsh DJ, Helms ER, Shaw G, Iraki J.** "Is an Energy Surplus Required to Maximize Skeletal Muscle Hypertrophy Associated With Resistance Training." *Front Nutr* 2019;6:131. DOI [10.3389/fnut.2019.00131](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnut.2019.00131). Narrative review. **~90 citations.** OA: gold. Concludes the specific surplus required is unknown; textbook surplus estimates "have never been validated in a resistance training population." — [Source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2019.00131/full)
- **Helms ER, Spence AJ, Sousa C, Kreiger J, Taylor S, Oranchuk DJ, Dieter BP, Watkins CM.** "Effect of Small and Large Energy Surpluses on Strength, Muscle, and Skinfold Thickness in Resistance-Trained Individuals: A Parallel Groups Design." *Sports Med Open* 2023;9:102. DOI [10.1186/s40798-023-00651-y](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-023-00651-y). RCT, 8 wk, maintenance vs +5% vs +15%, 17 completers. ~15 citations. OA: gold. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-023-00651-y)
  - Numbers: no group differences in muscle thickness or squat 1RM; +15% gained more bench 1RM and more skinfold thickness; body-mass change strongly predicted skinfold change (R²=0.49) and only weakly biceps MT (R²=0.24). Conclusion: faster weight gain "primarily increase[s] rates of fat gain." — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40798-023-00651-y)
- Garthe 2013 (elite athletes, surplus ~500 kcal/day with dietary counselling vs ad libitum, 8–12 wk): greater LBM gain but also greater fat gain in the surplus group (from full text knowledge; abstract not re-read). — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1080/17461391.2011.643923)

### Inferences
- App rule: beginner/intermediate +10–20% TDEE (≈ +250–500 kcal) targeting 0.25–0.5% BW/wk; advanced +5–10% (≈0.1–0.25% BW/wk). Prefer the low end for users concerned with fat gain. Protein 1.6–2.2 g/kg is sufficient in a surplus.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis of surplus size in trained lifters exists (searches for 2024–2025 SR found none). Helms 2023 is small (n=17, COVID-affected).

## Q6 — Estimating energy needs (BMR/RMR/TDEE equations)

### Takeaway
For the general (non-obese and obese) population, **Mifflin-St Jeor** is the most reliable equation (within ±10% of measured RMR for the most people; Frankenfield 2005). For lean, muscular athletes, FFM-based equations (**Cunningham**, Ten-Haaf) perform better; most body-weight equations **underestimate** RMR in physique athletes. Any equation is a starting point to be calibrated from weight trend.

### Cited Findings
- **Mifflin MD, St Jeor ST, Hill LA, Scott BJ, Daugherty SA, Koh YO.** "A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals." *Am J Clin Nutr* 1990;51(2):241–247. DOI [10.1093/ajcn/51.2.241](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/51.2.241). Original equation study. **~2,540 citations.** OA: closed. Equation (standard form): men RMR = 10×kg + 6.25×cm − 5×age + 5; women = same − 161. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/ajcn/51.2.241)
- **Frankenfield D, Roth-Yousey L, Compher C.** "Comparison of Predictive Equations for Resting Metabolic Rate in Healthy Nonobese and Obese Adults: A Systematic Review." *J Am Diet Assoc* 2005;105(5):775–789. DOI [10.1016/j.jada.2005.02.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jada.2005.02.005). ### Inferences
- App logic: if body-fat % available and user is lean/muscular → Cunningham (or Tinsley FFM equation for physique athletes); otherwise → Mifflin-St Jeor. Multiply by an activity factor for TDEE, then auto-correct after 2–3 weeks of weight-trend data (expected error ±10% or more per individual).

### Gaps
- Activity-factor (PAL) multipliers for TDEE were not researched here; no high-quality validation of PAL multipliers specific to RT populations was found in this session.
